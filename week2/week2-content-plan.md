## 1. Three unexpected signs your nervous system is overloaded

**Hook (on-screen, first 1s):** "3 signs your nervous system is overloaded (that have nothing to do with feeling stressed)"

**Script:**
You don't have to feel anxious for your nervous system to be maxed out. Watch for: a jaw that's tight before you've even opened your laptop. Feeling irritated by completely normal sounds — chewing, typing, a notification. And the third one people miss most: feeling bored and restless at the same time, unable to sit still but with zero energy to do anything either.

None of these mean something is wrong with you. They mean your body is asking for a pause before it has to ask louder.

## 2. Why rest sometimes feels uncomfortable

**Hook:** "Did you know rest can feel uncomfortable before it feels good?"

**Script:**
If lying down to rest makes you restless instead of relaxed, that's not you failing at resting. For a nervous system that's used to being "on," stillness can register as unsafe before it registers as safe — your body doesn't trust the quiet yet.

That discomfort usually isn't a sign to push through it or to give up on resting altogether. It's a sign to make the rest smaller and shorter at first, so your body can find out, in small doses, that nothing bad happens when it stops.

## 3. Five things that don't actually calm your nervous system

**Hook:** "5 things that feel productive but don't actually calm your nervous system"

**Script:**

## 4. The 30-second body reset

**Hook:** "A 30-second reset for when you don't have time to fully stop"

**Script:**
Feet flat on the floor. Shoulders drop, even if it's just half an inch. One slow exhale, longer than your inhale. Then gently press your palms together for a few seconds and release.

Thirty seconds won't fix a hard day. But it's enough to tell your body "we're not in danger right now," which is often the actual thing it's waiting to hear.

## 5. Stress doesn't only live in your head

**Hook:** "Gentle reminder: stress isn't just a thinking problem"

**Script:**
You can understand exactly why you're stressed, fully believe you should be fine, and still feel your chest tight and your stomach in knots. That's because stress is stored in the body, not just reasoned through in the mind.

This is why "just don't think about it" so rarely works. The body needs its own way back to calm — movement, breath, touch — separate from whatever your mind has already figured out.

## 6. Fight, flight, freeze, fawn — simply explained

**Hook:** "Fight, flight, freeze, fawn — what they actually look like"

**Script:**
Fight looks like irritability and snapping over small things. Flight looks like restlessness, overworking, needing to stay busy. Freeze looks like shutting down, going blank, feeling stuck. Fawn looks like people-pleasing, over-apologizing, saying yes when you mean no.

They're not personality traits. They're your nervous system's four ways of trying to keep you safe — and most people move through more than one of them in a single day.

## 7. The habit quietly keeping your nervous system switched on

**Hook:** "This one habit is quietly keeping your nervous system switched on"

**Script:**
Checking your phone in the first sixty seconds after waking up. Before your body has even settled into being awake, it's already taking in notifications, other people's opinions, and things to react to.

Your nervous system reads that as "we're needed, right now" before you've had a single moment of just being here. A few minutes of not reaching for it in the morning is a small change that changes how the whole day lands on your body.

## 8. Five ways stress quietly messes with your hormones

**Hook:** "5 ways stress quietly messes with your hormones"

**Script:**
It disrupts your sleep hormones, so you're tired but wired at bedtime. It affects your hunger cues, so you either lose your appetite or can't stop snacking. It can throw off your cycle. It raises cortisol, which over time affects your energy, mood, and focus. And it lowers the hormones connected to feeling calm and connected to others.

None of this means you did something wrong. It means your body is responding exactly the way it's designed to under sustained pressure.

## 9. Do this before you go to sleep

**Hook:** "Do this for two minutes before you go to sleep"

**Script:**
Lie down, hand on your belly. Breathe in through your nose for a count of four, out through your mouth for a count of six — exhale longer than the inhale, every time. That's it. That's the whole practice.

A longer exhale is one of the few things that reliably tells your nervous system it's safe to power down. Two minutes of this before sleep is often more effective than an hour of trying to "just relax."

**Music:** `diepthanhdk-rain-meditation-450575.mp3`

## 10. Things I stopped believing about rest

**Hook:** "Things I stopped believing about rest"

**Script:**
That it has to be earned. That it only counts if it's a whole day off. That being "productive" and being well are the same goal. That pushing through tiredness is discipline. And that rest is something you fit in after everything else is done, instead of something your body needs on the same level as food or water.

Unlearning these didn't happen overnight. It happened one small rest at a time, taken without asking permission first.

These aren't random. They're requests. Usually quiet ones, at first.

## 12. Nervous system myth vs fact

**Hook:** "Nervous system myth vs fact"

**Script:**

Myth: if you're not panicking, you're not dysregulated. Fact: numbness and shutdown are dysregulation too.

Myth: this is just how you are. Fact: nervous systems can and do recalibrate, at any age.
